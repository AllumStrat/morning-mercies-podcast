# Old Paths: where to research

Use WebSearch to find sources and WebFetch to read them. Quote only what you fetched. These are starting points, not limits.

## Reading the passages

- The ESV text is available at Bible Gateway: `https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah+36&version=ESV`. Read every verse of every reading in `work/today.json` before you research anything.

## Verse-by-verse classic commentary (public domain)

- Bible Hub gathers many classic commentaries on one page per verse: `https://biblehub.com/commentaries/jeremiah/36-23.htm`. It includes Matthew Henry, Gill, Poole, Barnes, Ellicott, the Pulpit Commentary, and others. Follow its links to the full text.
- John Calvin's commentaries are on CCEL (search "Calvin commentary Jeremiah ccel"). Calvin covers Jeremiah and Lamentations, the Psalms, the Gospels (as a Harmony, with John separately), and the epistles, including 1 Peter. He did not write commentaries on Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, or Song of Songs, so do not quote "Calvin on Ecclesiastes."
- Matthew Henry (Presbyterian nonconformist), John Gill (Baptist), Matthew Poole (Puritan), and John Trapp (Puritan) all cover the whole Bible. Bible Study Tools and StudyLight carry them.
- Spurgeon's Treasury of David is the essential Psalms source. It also quotes dozens of Puritans; verify each Puritan quote as Spurgeon prints it and attribute it as "quoted in Spurgeon's Treasury of David" when you cannot reach the original.

## Spurgeon

- Sermons by Scripture text: The Spurgeon Center (spurgeon.org), Spurgeon Gems (spurgeongems.org, which has a scripture index), and The Spurgeon Archive. Search `Spurgeon sermon "Jeremiah 36"`.
- Morning and Evening is also usable, and it often covers individual verses.

## Church fathers (public-domain translations)

- New Advent (newadvent.org/fathers) and CCEL host the Ante-Nicene and Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers.
- On John: Augustine's Tractates on the Gospel of John, Chrysostom's Homilies on John, and Cyril of Alexandria's Commentary on John.
- On Matthew: Chrysostom's Homilies on Matthew. On Acts: Chrysostom's Homilies on Acts. On the Pauline letters: Chrysostom's homilies on each.
- On the Psalms: Augustine's Expositions on the Psalms and Athanasius's Letter to Marcellinus.
- On Jeremiah: Jerome commented on chapters one through thirty-two. Patristic material on later chapters is thin, so use the fathers there only when you find something real, perhaps in a sermon or letter citing the verse.
- Gregory the Great, Ambrose, Basil, and Gregory of Nyssa appear across many books. Bernard of Clairvaux's Sermons on the Song of Songs were beloved by the Puritans and are fair game for Song of Songs.
- The Ancient Christian Commentary on Scripture can help you find which father commented on a verse, but verify the wording in a public-domain translation before quoting, or paraphrase it.

## Puritans and later Reformed and Presbyterian voices (public domain)

- John Owen, Thomas Watson, Richard Sibbes, Thomas Brooks, John Flavel, John Bunyan, Thomas Manton, Jeremiah Burroughs, Stephen Charnock, Jonathan Edwards, Thomas Boston, Samuel Rutherford, Robert Murray M'Cheyne, Charles Hodge, B.B. Warfield, J.C. Ryle (his Expository Thoughts on the Gospels is excellent on John), and Archibald Alexander.
- The Westminster Confession, Larger Catechism, and Shorter Catechism are useful when a doctrine is plainly in the text.
- Sources include CCEL, Monergism, the Banner of Truth website, and Grace Gems (gracegems.org, a Puritan library; verify author and work).

## Modern conservative voices (copyrighted, so use short quotes from pages you can fetch)

- R.C. Sproul: ligonier.org (articles, sermon series, and devotionals, often arranged by text) and the Reformation Study Bible notes where accessible.
- John Piper: desiringgod.org, with sermons and articles searchable by Scripture passage (search `site:desiringgod.org "Jeremiah 36"`), plus "Look at the Book" and "Ask Pastor John."
- Philip Graham Ryken (PCA): Jeremiah and Lamentations and Ecclesiastes (Preaching the Word series). Tenth Presbyterian Church sermon archives may have his sermons.
- James Montgomery Boice (PCA): sermons on John, the Psalms, and more through the Alliance of Confessing Evangelicals, Tenth Presbyterian, and others.
- Sinclair Ferguson, Derek Thomas, Ligon Duncan, and Kevin DeYoung: Ligonier, Reformed Theological Seminary, First Presbyterian Columbia, and The Gospel Coalition.
- Baptists: John MacArthur (gty.org sermon transcripts by passage), Thomas Schreiner (Southern Seminary and The Gospel Coalition), and Mark Dever (9Marks and Capitol Hill Baptist).
- Others: Alistair Begg (truthforlife.org), Martyn Lloyd-Jones (MLJ Trust), J.I. Packer, D.A. Carson (The Gospel Coalition), and Edmund Clowney.
- If a book cannot be fetched, do not quote it from memory. At most, say in general terms that an author takes a certain view, and only if you verified that elsewhere.

## Background and history

- For dates, kings, and archaeology, use reputable evangelical sources (Ligonier, The Gospel Coalition, Associates for Biblical Research, seminary sites) and Bible dictionaries hosted on Bible Hub and StudyLight. One relevant archaeological detail, such as the Lachish letters for Jeremiah, makes the setting vivid.
- For Hebrew and Greek, Bible Hub's interlinear and Strong's pages give basic word meanings.
