## 1. Setting and background

### Proverbs 26:1-16
- Proverbs 25:1 (ESV, esv.org): "These also are proverbs of Solomon which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied." So chapters 25-29 are Solomonic proverbs collected and edited in Hezekiah's court, around 700 B.C. (traditional, conservative view).
- Structure: verses 1-12 a cluster on the fool. Hebrew kesil (Strong's 3684, biblehub.com/hebrew/3684.htm): "Stupid fellow, dullard, fool"; root sense "fat", figuratively stupid; the page notes the term describes moral and spiritual obstinacy more than intellectual deficiency; about 70 OT occurrences, about 50 in Proverbs. It appears in every verse of 1-12 except verse 2.
- Verses 13-16 a cluster on the sluggard (Hebrew atsel), each verse beginning "The sluggard".
- Form: comparison proverbs, "Like... so..." with deliberately absurd images (snow in summer, rain at harvest; a lame man's dangling legs; a thorn in a drunkard's hand; a door on its hinges).
- Barnes' Notes (biblehub.com/commentaries/proverbs/26-4.htm): "The apparent contradiction between the two verses led some of the rabbis to question the canonical authority of this book." Mention in one sentence at most.

### Luke 23:26-31
- Follows Pilate's handing Jesus over (23:25). Luke's great journey section begins 9:51 "he set his face to go to Jerusalem." Jesus wept over the city (19:41-44) and warned in 21:20-24; 21:23 ESV: "Alas for women who are pregnant and for those who are nursing infants in those days! For there will be great distress upon the earth and wrath against this people." Fulfilled in the Roman siege, A.D. 70.
- Verses 27-31 are found only in Luke (parallels to v. 26: Matthew 27:32; Mark 15:21; John 19:17).
- Simon of Cyrene: Cyrene a city in North Africa (modern Libya) with a large Jewish community (Acts 2:10; 6:9; 11:20; 13:1). "Coming in from the country" (apo agrou, "from the field").
- Mark 15:21 (ESV): "And they compelled a passerby, Simon of Cyrene, who was coming in from the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to carry his cross." Romans 16:13 (ESV): "Greet Rufus, chosen in the Lord; also his mother, who has been a mother to me as well." Possibly the same family (Ferguson suggests it; say "possibly").
- Greek (biblehub.com/interlinear/luke/23-26.htm): epilabomenoi (1949) "laying hold of, seizing"; opisthen (3693) "behind, after". Luke alone says "behind Jesus" (Piper notes this).
- Luke 9:23 ESV: "If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me." Luke 14:27 ESV: "Whoever does not bear his own cross and come after me cannot be my disciple."
- Verse 30 quotes Hosea 10:8 (ESV): "and they shall say to the mountains, 'Cover us,' and to the hills, 'Fall on us.'" Hosea's original: judgment on Samaria and its high places. Also Revelation 6:16.
- Verse 31 Greek (biblehub.com/interlinear/luke/23-31.htm): hygro xylo, "green, wet, sappy" wood (5200, lexicon "Moist, green, full of sap"); xero "dry, withered" (3584). Ezekiel 20:47 ESV: "I will kindle a fire in you, and it shall devour every green tree in you and every dry tree." Several commentators (Barnes, Ellicott, Gill, Bengel) treat it as a proverbial saying.

### Hebrews 9:11-28
- Central section of the letter, 8:1 to 10:18: Christ's high priesthood and sacrifice, superior to the Levitical system. 9:1-10 described the earthly tent and the Day of Atonement. 10:1-18 (tomorrow) completes the argument. 8:13: the first covenant "is becoming obsolete and growing old is ready to vanish away", likely written before A.D. 70, temple still standing (traditional conservative view).
- Leviticus 16:34 ESV: "that atonement may be made for the people of Israel once in the year because of all their sins."
- Red heifer: Numbers 19; Numbers 19:9 ashes kept "for the water for impurity", "it is a sin offering"; for cleansing after contact with a corpse.
- Exodus 24:8 ESV: "Behold the blood of the covenant that the Lord has made with you in accordance with all these words."
- Greek: ephapax (2178) "once for all" in 9:12; hapax (530) "once" in 9:26, 27, 28; lytrosis (3085) redemption, ransoming; apekdechomai (553) "await eagerly" in 9:28 (biblehub interlinear 9:12 and 9:28).
- Isaiah 53:12 ESV: "yet he bore the sin of many, and makes intercession for the transgressors." Hebrews 9:28 "to bear the sins of many" alludes to it.
- Diatheke: covenant and will (Calvin on 9:16).

## 2. Outlines of the passages

### Proverbs 26:1-16 (ESV via biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+26%3A1-16&version=ESV)
- 1-3: honor does not fit a fool (snow in summer); causeless curse does not alight (sparrow, swallow); the rod for the back of fools.
- 4-5: answer not / answer a fool according to his folly.
- 6-10: the fool as messenger ("cuts off his own feet and drinks violence"); proverb in a fool's mouth (lame man's legs, thorn in a drunkard's hand); honoring a fool like binding a stone in a sling; archer who wounds everyone.

- 12: "Do you see a man who is wise in his own eyes? There is more hope for a fool than for him." (Key turn.)
- 13-16: the sluggard: lion in the road; door on its hinges; hand in the dish; "wiser in his own eyes than seven men who can answer sensibly."

### Luke 23:26-31 (ESV via biblegateway)
- 26: "they seized one Simon of Cyrene, who was coming in from the country, and laid on him the cross, to carry it behind Jesus."
- 27: great multitude, women mourning and lamenting.
- 28: "Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for me, but weep for yourselves and for your children."
- 29: "Blessed are the barren and the wombs that never bore and the breasts that never nursed!"
- 30: mountains, fall on us; hills, cover us.
- 31: "For if they do these things when the wood is green, what will happen when it is dry?"

### Hebrews 9:11-28 (ESV via biblegateway)
- 11-14: greater tent not made with hands; "he entered once for all into the holy places, not by means of the blood of goats and calves but by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption"; "how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God, purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God."
- 15-22: mediator of a new covenant; will takes effect at death; first covenant inaugurated with blood (Moses, Exodus 24); "without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins."
- 23-26: heavenly things purified with better sacrifices; "into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf"; not repeatedly; "he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself."
- 27-28: "just as it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment, so Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him."

## 3. Interpretive questions

### Proverbs
1. How do 26:4 and 26:5 fit? Poole: by persons, times, circumstances. Sproul: the answer depends on the situation; verse 5 as reductio ad absurdum. Henry: fit remarks to the man and address his conscience. McDowell (Tenth Presbyterian): do not stoop to his level; sometimes folly must be exposed. Barnes: rabbis questioned the book's canonicity over it.
2. Why is the man wise in his own eyes worse than the fool (v. 12)? Poole: thinks himself wise and scorns counsel. Pulpit Commentary: self-conceit shuts the door to improvement. Verse 16 shows the sluggard is this man too.
3. What is the lion (v. 13)? Henry: only in his own fancy. Gill: small inconveniences painted as lions; the real lion is Satan. Spurgeon: laziness is a lion-maker.

### Luke
1. Who carried the cross, Jesus (John 19:17) or Simon? Augustine: both; Jesus first, then Simon for the rest of the way.
2. Why does Jesus turn away the women's sympathy? Calvin: not a reproof of tears as such but a warning that there will be greater cause; his death is not the end but the beginning of evils to Jerusalem; and it shows the Father's care. Spurgeon: lament the disease, not the remedy; Spurgeon even says bless these women for their sympathy, but fears their emotion was fleeting. Flavel: melting affections are not infallible signs of grace. DeYoung: his seeming insensitivity is the deepest expression of love.
3. What does green and dry wood mean? (a) Romans treating the innocent so, what of the rebellious nation (Theophylact, Pulpit); (b) God's wrath falling on the innocent Son, what of sinners (Henry, Sproul); (c) Calvin gives two readings and says the general sense is the same. Gill: the green tree expresses the righteousness and innocence of Christ.

### Hebrews
1. What are dead works? Chrysostom: as touching a corpse defiled, so touching a dead work defiles the conscience. Calvin: works that produce death or are fruits of death. Spurgeon: dead works are not necessarily wilful sins but acts not done from spiritual life.
2. Will or covenant in 9:16-17? Calvin: Greek diatheke includes both; the writer alludes to its secondary meaning; proof the letter was not written in Hebrew.
3. "The sins of many" (9:28): Chrysostom: why of many not all, because not all believed. Calvin: many means all as in Romans 5:15, but the point is he died for others, not himself, "he opposes many to one". A surprising agreement between Chrysostom and Calvin; not to be debated on air.
